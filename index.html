A QUICK INTRODUCTION

One subject.
Different kinds of text.

Effective communication means sharing information in a way that people can understand. Technical texts usually help readers do something correctly, while academic texts explain ideas, share knowledge, or discuss research. Look at each example and notice who it is for, why it was written, and where it is used.

TECHNICAL TEXT

01 / GUIDE

Installation Guide

An installation guide provides step-by-step instructions on how to set up a product properly. It guides users through the process and helps prevent mistakes during installation.

A Audience

People who will install the product, such as technicians, installers, and users.

P Purpose

To guide users in installing a product correctly and safely by following the proper steps.

C Context

Used when installing a new product or equipment that needs to be set up properly.

TECHNICAL TEXT

02 / GUIDE

Maintenance Guide

A maintenance guide explains how to take care of equipment to keep it working properly. A car maintenance guide may cover engine oil, coolant, filters, and other parts that need regular care.

A Audience

Vehicle owners, mechanics, technicians, and people responsible for maintaining equipment.

P Purpose

To help users maintain equipment, prevent problems, and make it last longer.

C Context

Used during regular inspections, cleaning, and replacement of parts.

TECHNICAL TEXT

03 / GUIDE

Safety Guide

A safety guide gives rules and reminders that help people avoid accidents and injuries. Examples include wearing protective equipment, keeping the workplace clean, and reporting unsafe conditions.

A Audience

Workers, students, technicians, and anyone working with tools, machines, or equipment.

P Purpose

To prevent accidents and injuries by reminding people to follow proper safety procedures.

C Context

Used in workplaces, workshops, laboratories, and other areas where safety precautions are needed.

TECHNICAL TEXT

04 / REFERENCE

Technical Datasheet

A technical datasheet contains important product information, including specifications, features, and performance. For paint, it may list drying time, coverage, thickness, and application instructions.

A Audience

Engineers, technicians, contractors, painters, and people who need detailed product information.

P Purpose

To provide accurate technical details so users can understand specifications and use a product correctly.

C Context

Used when selecting a product, checking specifications, planning an application, or comparing products.

TECHNICAL TEXT

05 / MANUAL

User Manual

A user manual explains how to operate and use a product correctly. A camera quick-start guide may show how to attach the strap, charge and insert the battery, attach the lens, and insert a memory card.

A Audience

Camera owners, beginners, photographers, and anyone who wants to learn how to use the camera.

P Purpose

To help users understand product functions and learn how to operate a product properly.

C Context

Used when using a new camera, learning its basic functions, or checking instructions.

01 / TEXTBOOK

Biological Molecules

Topic 2.3 · Concepts of Biology

This textbook section explains carbon and the major types of biological macromolecules, including their structures and functions. It presents scientific knowledge, definitions, classifications, and explanations for educational purposes.

Summary

Biological macromolecules are large organic molecules essential for life. The four major types are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids.

  • Carbon: Forms four covalent bonds and can create chains, branches, and rings.
  • Carbohydrates: Provide energy and structural support. They include monosaccharides, disaccharides, and polysaccharides.
  • Lipids: Store long-term energy, provide insulation, and help form cell membranes.
  • Proteins: Made of amino acids and help with structure, chemical reactions, hormones, transport, and protection.
  • Nucleic acids: DNA stores genetic information, while RNA helps use it to make proteins.

A Audience

Students, teachers, and science learners studying biology, chemistry, and life processes.

P Purpose

To inform and explain the structure, classification, properties, and functions of biological molecules.

C Context

Science education, particularly biology and biochemistry.

DICTIONARY

02 / DICTIONARY

Climate Change

Cambridge Dictionary example

This reference text gives the meaning of climate change and examples of language use. It helps readers understand the term and use it correctly in academic discussions and written assignments.

Summary

Climate change refers to long-term changes in Earth's average temperature, rainfall, wind patterns, and other climate conditions. Although climate can change naturally, recent rapid global warming is mainly caused by human activities.

Burning fossil fuels releases greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide. Deforestation, industry, transportation, and some farming practices also contribute to emissions.

A Audience

Students, teachers, language learners, and general readers.

P Purpose

To define and clarify the meaning of climate change and help readers use the term correctly.

C Context

Language learning, science education, and environmental studies.

ENCYCLOPEDIA

03 / ENCYCLOPEDIA

The Solar System

Encyclopaedia Britannica

This encyclopedia entry presents general scientific information about the Sun and the celestial objects that revolve around it. It organizes astronomy knowledge for educational purposes.

Summary

The solar system consists of the Sun and objects that orbit it, including eight planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, and comets. The Sun produces energy through nuclear fusion, providing light and heat to Earth.

The planets in order from the Sun are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Gravity keeps planets and other objects in orbit. Studying the solar system helps explain planetary motion and the formation of celestial bodies.

A Audience

Students, teachers, astronomy learners, and general readers.

P Purpose

To inform and explain the structure and components of the solar system.

C Context

Astronomy education, space science, and scientific reference research.

RESEARCH PAPER

04 / RESEARCH PAPER

Microplastics in the Environment

A Review of Current Understanding and Research Needs

A research paper presents scientific information gathered through research, evidence, and analysis. This paper discusses microplastics, their sources and distribution, possible environmental effects, and the need for further research.

Summary

Microplastics are tiny plastic particles generally smaller than five millimeters. They can come from broken-down plastic products, synthetic textile fibers, tire wear, and industrial processes.

They have been found in oceans, freshwater, soil, and other environments. Some organisms may ingest them, but the level of risk varies and some effects remain uncertain. Better sampling methods and less plastic waste can help researchers understand and reduce pollution.

A Audience

Environmental scientists, researchers, university students, and environmental policymakers.

P Purpose

To review scientific knowledge about microplastics and identify important research questions.

C Context

Environmental science, pollution research, and ecosystem protection.

SCIENTIFIC JOURNAL

05 / SCIENTIFIC JOURNAL

The Extraordinary Importance of Sleep

The Detrimental Effects of Inadequate Sleep on Health and Public Safety

A scientific journal article communicates research findings and explanations about a subject. This article discusses why sleep supports normal body functions, thinking, and everyday activities.

Summary

Sleep is a natural biological state that supports physical health, learning, memory, attention, and emotional regulation. Not getting enough sleep can reduce concentration, slow reactions, and make decision-making more difficult.

Fatigue can also increase the chance of mistakes during tasks that require alertness. Regular sleep and wake times, a comfortable sleeping environment, and enough time for rest can support healthy sleep habits. Sufficient sleep is especially important for teenagers as their bodies and brains continue to develop.

A Audience

Students, healthcare professionals, researchers, educators, and general readers.

P Purpose

To explain the importance of sleep and describe the consequences of inadequate sleep for health and safety.

C Context

Health education, sleep research, public health, and safety awareness.
